# Introduction

## Rationale

[cite Campbell's Operative Orthopedics chapter on ACL grafts here]

Although non-invasive surgical techniques have developed alongside technological advancements and their potential to supplant non-native ACL graft harvesting reconstruction does seem promising, modern practice will benefit from having an evidence-based review that sums up comparisons between all available grafts from all available literature to date. If in fact there is no difference in outcome on the basis of graft choice, orthopedic surgeons and biomedical engineers may deem more constructive to focus on cooperation to manufacture synthetic grafts, or physical therapists and physiotherapists to strengthen rehabilitation protocols instead, for example. As current recent has suggested that there exists an overall plateau or a "glass ceiling" so to speak, that exemplifies the stagnation and dissatisfaction of those who are more likely to face osteoarthritic changes and total knee arthroplasty rather than returning to pre-injury athletic ability.   

## Objectives

The objective for this review article was to provide a comprehensive, yet detailed overview on the quantitative effects that graft choice by the surgeon has on patient outcomes in anterior cruciate ligament reconstruction surgery. In regards to patient outcomes after ACLR, recent studies have suggested two things: 1. there is improvement in patient outcomes following ACLR over these years, and 2. there is no significant difference in outcomes between grafts. Therefore, if continued improvement is to continue over the next years, with the goal of reaching restorative treatment effects, where the athlete returns to preinjurious state, then the direction in which these advancements must head, is an important question to answer.
